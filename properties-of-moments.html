SECTION 01Properties of Sub-Gaussian Moments and MGFs

We introduce some additional properties of sub-Gaussian moments that will be useful later.

Claim · MGF Control and Moment Control Are Equivalent

Let X have mean zero. The following two conditions are equivalent:

(1) There is a σ > 0 such that

E[eλX]≤eλ2σ22,λ∈R

(2) There is a θ > 0 such that

E[X2k]≤2(2θ2)kk!,k=1,2,…

Controlling the sub-Gaussian MGF therefore controls every even moment. Conversely, this growth bound on all even moments implies sub-Gaussianity. The proof below gives (1) → (2) with θ = σ; the parameter may change in the reverse direction.

Proof · MGF Control Implies Moment Control

Although differentiating the MGF at zero recovers moments, an inequality between functions does not imply the same inequality between their derivatives. We instead pass through tail probabilities.

The MGF bound gives the sub-Gaussian tail bound

P(|X|≥t)≤2e−t22σ2,t≥0

Relate the moment to the tail using

E[X2k]=∫0∞2kt2k−1P(|X|≥t)dt

For a nonnegative random variable Y,

E[Y]=∫0∞P(Y>u)du

Think of Y as a stack of layers. If Y takes values 0, 1, 2, or 3, then a value of 1 contributes one layer, 2 contributes two layers, and 3 contributes three layers. Thus E[Y] = P(Y ≥ 1) + P(Y ≥ 2) + P(Y ≥ 3). A value of 3 is counted three times because it contributes 3 to the average. For continuous values, the layers become an integral. Set Y = |X|²ᵏ and substitute u = t²ᵏ to obtain the identity above.

Substitute the tail bound and integrate:

E[X2k]≤4k∫0∞t2k−1e−t22σ2dt=2(2σ2)kk!

SECTION 02Gaussian Comparison

Claim · MGF, Gaussian Tail Comparison, and Moment Control Are Equivalent

Let X have mean zero. The following three conditions are equivalent:

(1) There is a σ > 0 such that

E[eλX]≤eλ2σ22,λ∈R

(2) There is a constant c ≥ 1 and a Gaussian variable Z ∼ N(0, τ²), with τ > 0, such that

P(|X|≥s)≤cP(|Z|≥s),s≥0

A sub-Gaussian variable is dominated, in a certain sense, by a Gaussian variable: its tail probability is bounded by a constant times a Gaussian tail probability.

(3) There is a θ > 0 such that

E[X2k]≤(2k)!2kk!θ2k,k=1,2,…

Like condition (2) in Section 1, this condition controls the tail indirectly by bounding every even moment. Here, the moment bounds have the same form as the moments of a zero-mean Gaussian variable with scale θ.

All three conditions characterize sub-Gaussianity. The parameters may change when passing between the conditions.

Proof · Gaussian Tail Comparison Implies Moment Control

Applying the same tail-integral identity gives

E[X2k]≤cE[Z2k]=c(2k)!2kk!τ2k

The constant c can be absorbed into the moment parameter by taking θ = τ√c.

For Z ∼ N(0, τ²), odd moments vanish because the distribution is symmetric about zero. Its even moments are

E[Z2k]=(2k)!2kk!τ2k

For example, E[Z²] = τ², E[Z⁴] = 3τ⁴, and E[Z⁶] = 15τ⁶. The coefficient in the sub-Gaussian moment bound is therefore exactly the Gaussian moment coefficient.

SECTION 03Connecting the Two Moment Bounds

Sections 1 and 2 describe the same type of moment control, with different constants. For k ≥ 1, we have 2(k!)² ≤ (2k)!, so the bound from Section 1 gives

E[X2k]≤2(2σ2)kk!≤(2k)!2kk!(2σ)2k

Taking θ = 2σ puts this bound in the Gaussian moment form used in Section 2. The parameters may differ, but both forms characterize sub-Gaussian moment growth.
