SECTION 01Properties of Sub-Gaussian Moments and MGFs

We introduce some additional properties of sub-Gaussian moments that will be useful later.

Claim · MGF Control and Moment Control Are Equivalent

Let X have mean zero. The following two conditions are equivalent:

(1) There is a σ > 0 such that

E[eλX]≤eλ2σ22,λ∈R

(2) There is a θ > 0 such that

E[X2k]≤(2k)!2kk!θ2k,k=1,2,…

Controlling the sub-Gaussian MGF therefore controls every even moment. Conversely, this growth bound on all even moments implies sub-Gaussianity. The parameters may change when passing between the conditions.

Although differentiating the MGF at zero recovers moments, an inequality between functions does not imply the same inequality between their derivatives. We instead pass through tail probabilities.

Proof · MGF Control Implies Moment Control

The MGF bound gives the sub-Gaussian tail bound

P(|X|≥t)≤2e−t22σ2,t≥0

Relate the moment to the tail using

E[X2k]=∫0∞2kt2k−1P(|X|≥t)dt

For a nonnegative random variable Y,

E[Y]=∫0∞P(Y>u)du

Think of Y as a stack of layers. If Y takes values 0, 1, 2, or 3, then a value of 1 contributes one layer, 2 contributes two layers, and 3 contributes three layers. Thus E[Y] = P(Y ≥ 1) + P(Y ≥ 2) + P(Y ≥ 3). A value of 3 is counted three times because it contributes 3 to the average. For continuous values, the layers become an integral. Set Y = |X|²ᵏ and substitute u = t²ᵏ to obtain the identity above.

Substitute the tail bound and integrate:

E[X2k]≤4k∫0∞t2k−1e−t22σ2dt=2(2σ2)kk!

For k ≥ 1, we have 2(k!)² ≤ (2k)!. Hence

E[X2k]≤2(2σ2)kk!≤(2k)!2kk!(2σ)2k

Taking θ = 2σ gives the stated moment bound.

SECTION 02Gaussian Comparison

Claim · Comparison with a Gaussian

For a zero-mean X, sub-Gaussianity is also equivalent to the existence of a constant c ≥ 1 and a Gaussian variable Z ∼ N(0, τ²), with τ > 0, such that

P(|X|≥s)≤cP(|Z|≥s),s≥0

In this sense, the tail of X is dominated by a Gaussian tail. Applying the same tail-integral identity gives

E[X2k]≤cE[Z2k]=c(2k)!2kk!τ2k

The constant c can be absorbed into the moment parameter. This is the book’s route from Gaussian tail domination to moment control.

Example · Gaussian Moments

For Z ∼ N(0, τ²), odd moments vanish because the distribution is symmetric about zero. Its even moments are

E[Z2k]=(2k)!2kk!τ2k

For example, E[Z²] = τ², E[Z⁴] = 3τ⁴, and E[Z⁶] = 15τ⁶. The coefficient in the sub-Gaussian moment bound is therefore exactly the Gaussian moment coefficient.
